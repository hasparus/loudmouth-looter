# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

TTRPG players and DMs, mostly strangers at a convention table deciding in
seconds whether to sit down. Secondary: itch.io browsers and blog readers who
want the printable version of the "Why Would You Fight a Dragon?" ruleset. They
hold a folded A4 (four A5 pages) in their hands; it must work in print, on
paper, at arm's length.

## Product Purpose

A one-sheet printable PbtA microgame: front cover / invitation, inside rules
spread, and Hunter playbook. Success = a stranger understands the premise
instantly from the cover, and the sheet is playable at the table with no other
material. Playable first, beautiful second.

## Brand Personality

Doom-broadside. Grim, direct, medieval. Blades in the Dark information design +
MÖRK BORG emotional force + medieval manuscript/woodcut surface. No jokes, no
asides, no winks — humor only as deadpan register drops.

## Anti-references

- Fake parchment overkill, decorative clutter
- Equal-box spreadsheet layouts, modern UI cards
- Every move getting an illustration
- Handwritten marginal jokes
- "A game of goals, sacrifice and doom"-style subtitle copy

## Design Principles

- Rules behave like an interface: repeated syntax identical, most-used info
  where the eye returns.
- One violent dominant visual per surface; bold asymmetry, dramatic scale
  shifts. Not everything equally polite.
- Medieval marginalia as navigation and texture, never as content: rubricated
  headings, decorated initials, green vines.
- Two faces only: Avara Black (display, move names, initials, result numbers)
  and Crimson Text (body, italic triggers).
- Crimson = danger (titles, move names, Pyre, results); deep green = structure
  and ornament; black ink on off-white paper.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Print-first: body text ≥ readable at 10pt equivalent, never sacrifice usability
for gothic flavor. Screen version keeps semantic HTML, dark mode via existing
site scheme, and prints correctly (A4 landscape imposition).
